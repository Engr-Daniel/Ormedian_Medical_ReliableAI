Create this presentation using the established visual identity for the
OR-MEDIAN Reliable AI Research Training program.

VISUAL IDENTITY
===============

The presentation must have a clean, modern, academic/research-oriented
visual style inspired by the Ormedian brand.

The slides should feel like a professional AI research lab presentation,
not a corporate marketing deck and not a generic classroom PowerPoint.

COLOUR SYSTEM
=============

Use a restrained colour system based on the Ormedian identity:
blue shades supported by black and white.

PRIMARY:
Ormedian Blue family — blue is not limited to a single shade or to
lighter/darker versions of the logo blue. Deep blue, navy, royal blue,
electric blue, azure and sky blue are all welcome.

Use the blue in the supplied Ormedian logo as a brand reference while
choosing a small, coordinated selection of blue shades for each deck.
Multiple blue shades may appear together on a slide where they help
explain the content or establish visual hierarchy.

NEUTRALS:
Black / near-black — primary body text, diagrams and strong contrast.
White — primary slide background and negative space.

Use blue selectively for:
- key concepts
- headings or heading accents
- arrows
- important nodes in diagrams
- highlighted keywords
- section identifiers
- important numbers/results

Use black/near-black for:
- normal text
- diagram labels
- supporting information

Use white as the dominant background.

Do NOT introduce unrelated accent colours unless the content genuinely
requires semantic distinction.

Suggested roles for blue shades:
- deep blue or navy for headings and strong emphasis
- royal blue, electric blue or azure for key concepts, arrows and highlights
- sky blue or pale blue for supporting diagram fills and subtle panels

These are flexible roles, not a requirement to use every shade. Keep
the role of each chosen shade consistent throughout a deck. Neutral
greys may also support the palette.

Maintain clear contrast between text and its background. Use dark text
on light blue fills; avoid pale blue text on white. Do not rely on subtle
shade differences alone to distinguish concepts or data series: add
labels, shapes or line styles where needed.

The deck should remain recognizably blue + black + white throughout.

TYPOGRAPHY
==========

Primary presentation font:
QUATTROCENTO

Use Quattrocento for:
- slide titles
- section titles
- body text
- quotations
- major labels
- explanatory text

Technical / monospace font:
COURIER NEW

Use Courier New only for:
- code
- equations when monospace presentation is appropriate
- tensor shapes
- variable names
- file paths
- experiment IDs
- commands
- short technical annotations

Do not use Courier New for ordinary paragraphs.

Maintain a strong visual hierarchy:
- large, prominent titles
- medium-sized supporting statements
- readable body text
- small text only when unavoidable

Never shrink text merely to fit too much content onto a slide.
Reduce content instead.

LAYOUT
======

Use 16:9 widescreen format.

Maintain generous white space.

Prefer:
- one main idea per slide
- short statements rather than paragraphs
- strong alignment
- balanced margins
- simple visual hierarchy
- visual explanation over excessive text

Avoid:
- crowded slides
- decorative clutter
- excessive borders
- unnecessary gradients
- excessive shadows
- generic PowerPoint templates
- walls of bullet points

The presentation should look intentionally designed.

BRANDING
========

Use the supplied Ormedian logo consistently.

Place it subtly, preferably:
- top-right, or
- bottom-right

Keep its placement and size consistent across ordinary slides.

The title slide may use a larger logo.

Do not distort, recolour, crop, stretch or recreate the logo.

Do not make the logo compete with the educational content.

ILLUSTRATION STYLE
==================

The slides should be highly illustrative.

Whenever a concept can be explained visually, prefer:

- diagrams
- process flows
- conceptual models
- comparison layouts
- timelines
- simple system architectures
- experiment pipelines
- annotated examples
- cause-and-effect diagrams

over long textual explanations.

Illustrations should follow the same visual language:

white background
+
black text/structure
+
Ormedian blue-family emphasis (one or more coordinated blue shades)

Use simple geometric shapes, clean arrows and minimal line icons.

Avoid random stock imagery and decorative illustrations that do not
improve understanding.

For AI/medical-imaging concepts, diagrams should be scientifically
meaningful rather than merely attractive.

RESEARCH PRESENTATION STYLE
===========================

The deck is part of a research-training program.

Therefore clearly distinguish concepts such as:

Observation
Interpretation
Hypothesis
Experiment
Evidence
Conclusion

When showing workflows, use directional visual structure.

When comparing concepts, prefer side-by-side visual comparisons.

When showing an experiment, visually identify:
Question → Setup → Measurement → Result → Interpretation.

When presenting quantitative results, emphasize the important number
without exaggerating its significance.

Do not visually imply stronger scientific conclusions than the
evidence supports.

TEACH-BACK PRINCIPLE
====================

These slides represent the trainee's understanding of the lecture.

Do NOT simply convert lecture-note paragraphs into slides.

Instead:

1. identify the core idea,
2. explain it in the trainee's own words,
3. visualize the mental model,
4. provide a concise example,
5. connect it to Reliable AI / Medical Imaging where appropriate.

The presentation should demonstrate understanding, not transcription.

SLIDE RHYTHM
============

Where appropriate, structure a learning presentation approximately as:

1. Title
2. Why this matters
3. Core mental model
4. Key concepts
5. Visual explanation
6. Example
7. Research/technical application
8. Common mistake or failure mode
9. Personal understanding / reflection
10. Key takeaway

The exact number of slides should depend on the content.

Do not artificially force every presentation into ten slides.
